# Code of conduct

## Our pledge

This project is built in the open, and the people who read,
use and contribute to it should be able to do so without
harassment or hostility. We pledge to keep every space around
this project — issues, pull requests, discussions and any
project channel — welcoming for everyone, whatever their age,
background, experience level, nationality, or identity.

## The standard, in plain words

Behaviour that helps:

- Be kind and patient, especially with people who are new.
- Criticise the code, the design or the claim — never the
  person. "This diagnosis cites no evidence" is welcome;
  remarks about the author are not.
- Assume good faith first. Most sharp wording is a rushed
  message, not an attack; ask before escalating.
- Give credit where it is due, and accept correction the
  same way: by checking the evidence.

Behaviour that is not accepted:

- Harassment, insults, or personal attacks of any kind.
- Sexualised language or attention.
- Publishing someone else's private information (an address,
  a phone number, a private message) without their clear
  permission.
- Deliberately derailing, spamming, or flooding project
  spaces.
- Any conduct that would reasonably make another person feel
  unsafe taking part.

## Scope

This code applies in all project spaces, and when someone is
representing the project in public — for example, speaking
about it or posting as the project.

## Enforcement

The maintainer (Abraham Kakooza) is responsible for applying
this code. Reports can be made privately through his GitHub
profile. Every report will be read; what was shared in
confidence stays in confidence.

Responses are proportionate to what happened, and can
include: a private note, a public warning, asking for an
edit or an apology, closing or removing a comment or pull
request, or — for repeated or serious behaviour — blocking
the person from the project's spaces. A first, minor slip
that is corrected when pointed out is treated as exactly
that: a slip.
